Budgeting is harder when income changes every month, but the answer is not to stop planning. It is to separate the money you can count on from the money that is still uncertain.

## Choose a planning baseline

Look at recent take-home income and choose a conservative monthly baseline. Use that number for essential expenses. When income is higher, give the extra money a job instead of quietly increasing every category.

## Separate essential and flexible spending

Rent, utilities, food, insurance, and minimum debt payments come first. Entertainment, shopping, and optional upgrades can expand only after essentials and the month’s priorities are covered.

## Use strong months to protect weak months

Extra income can build a cash buffer, fund annual bills, or pay down expensive debt. That buffer smooths future months so one low paycheck does not immediately force a difficult decision.

## Budget by priority when money arrives

If pay comes in several times a month, assign each deposit to the next important need: upcoming bills, food, transportation, savings, and then flexible spending. This is more practical than pretending every month begins with the same amount.

## Review the baseline regularly

Your conservative number should reflect reality, not punish you forever. Review it after several months of income and expenses, then adjust the plan deliberately.
